Gramly's design system

Ink

Gramly draws on top of other people's apps, so it stays quiet. It uses neutral surfaces, black or white for the main action, and colour only where colour means something. Nothing here belongs to macOS, so the Linux surface draws the same thing.

Appearance
Values: tokens.json · theme.rs · DESIGN.md

Principles

  • Quiet on top of anythingNeutral surfaces in the system's light or dark. Underlines look the same in both, because the app underneath can be either.
  • Colour means wrong or rightRed is a mistake and the words it takes out. Blue is clearer wording. Green is what a fix puts in. Colour never means "click here".
  • One of eachOne card for every suggestion, one button size and one motion. A new kind of suggestion fits the card; it doesn't get its own.
  • The same everywhereGramly brings its own font and shadow. It uses no system accent, no vibrancy and no SF font, so a Mac and Hyprland look alike.

Colour

Every colour Gramly draws, in both appearances. The card and the dictionary follow the system; the colours under Both are drawn over other apps and never change.

The card's shadow

Gramly draws its own, in a panel that ignores the pointer, so it looks the same on Linux and clicks beside the card reach the text. It has a half-point hairline (edge), a tight shadow at the edge (drop 1, blur 2) and a soft one below (inset 12, drop 14, blur 18). It's stronger in Dark (0.7) than in Light (0.2), because a shadow barely shows on a dark app. The dictionary uses the system's window shadow for now.

Motion

Only two things move. The card fades in over 140ms, moving its last 3 points towards its word. A word you accept glows green for 380ms. Nothing else animates, not even a button under the pointer.

Underline

Two points high under the word, in red for a mistake or blue for clearer wording. While the word's card is open, the word also gets a tint of the same colour at 16%. It's the same in light and dark apps.

The change

Inside the card, the sentence shows the change in place: what goes is struck, what comes is bold. Each word gets its colour in one place only. The old word's letters and line are red; the new word is green on a green fill.

✓Do

Did you recieve receive the photos?

Red letters with a line in the same red, then green on green. Each word is one mark.

✕Don't

Did you recieve receive the photos?

Pale red letters under a darker red line. In Dark, the two reds blur together.

✕Don't

Did you recieve receive the photos?

Plain letters with the colour only in the line and the fill. The change no longer stands out from the sentence.

With red-green colour blindness the two colours can look alike. The strike and the bold still show which word goes and which comes.

Card

One card for every suggestion. From top to bottom: a dot in the underline's colour and a title, the sentence with the change in place, then a row of buttons with the main one first. Spelling adds "Add to dictionary". Two or more fixes in a sentence become "Correct the sentence". A change of spaces alone shows as ␣. The ⋯ at the title's end opens the card's menu.

Button

Every button is 32 high with 6 corners. The main button is solid ink. The others are outlined, and the outline gives way to a fill under the pointer. There's no pressed state: the click acts at once and the card closes.

Dictionary

Opened from the menu. It's a borderless window with Gramly's own header and close cross, the card's surface, and the same buttons. It has a field to add a word and a list to remove one from. Escape and Ctrl/⌘-W close it.

Files

  • design/tokens.json: every value on this page, for the code and for the Linux surface.
  • src/macos/theme.rs: colours, corners and the font. Card, button and dictionary measures are in overlay.rs and dictionary.rs.
  • cargo test fails when the code and tokens.json disagree, or when this page leaves out a colour. Change both together.
  • gramly preview: draws every card and the dictionary to PNG in both appearances, from the app's own code. This page is a close copy; the previews are the real thing.
  • DESIGN.md: the short version, next to the code.
